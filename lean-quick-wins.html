Lean Manufacturing: Quick Wins for Small Teams

You do not need a lean transformation programme to get real results. Five small, practical changes you can start this week, with the detail behind each one.

3 min readOperations

01Run a 5S pass on one area

5S stands for Sort, Straighten, Shine, Standardise, Sustain. Pick one workbench or cell, not the whole factory, and work through each step: remove what is not needed, arrange what remains logically, clean it properly, agree a standard for how it should look, and build a habit of maintaining it. Start this week, on one small area, and let success there build the case for doing more.

02Put up a simple visual KPI board

One whiteboard, three or four numbers that actually matter, such as output, quality or on-time delivery, updated daily or weekly where the whole team can see it, not just management. The value is not sophistication, it is visibility: problems get noticed and acted on faster when everyone can see the trend, rather than reading about it in a meeting.

03Write SOPs for your top three recurring problems

Not everything needs a written procedure. Just the three issues that cost you the most time or cause the most rework. A one-page standard operating procedure that a new starter could follow without asking for help is worth more than a comprehensive manual nobody reads.

04Use a simple root-cause method

Ask "why" five times on your next defect or delay before acting. Most first answers describe a symptom, not the actual cause. This costs nothing but a few minutes of discipline, and it prevents the same problem recurring next month in a slightly different form.

A worked exampleDefect found → why? Wrong tolerance set → why? Operator used the wrong job sheet → why? Job sheets are not clearly labelled by revision → why? No standard for revision control on the shop floor. The fix here is a labelling standard, not just retraining one operator.

05Hold a 10-minute daily huddle

Same time, same three questions: what went well, what did not, what needs help. Kept strictly to ten minutes and standing up if possible, this catches small problems while they are still small, rather than letting them build into a bigger issue by the end of the week.

06Why buyers value this

Buyers awarding regulated or safety-critical work look for suppliers who improve steadily and can show it. Small, evidenced changes that you sustain week after week say more about how you will perform on a contract than a one-off, big-bang transformation that is hard to keep going. The habit of noticing a problem, fixing its cause and holding on to the gain is exactly what a serious customer wants to see, and it is visible in your KPI board, your huddle notes and your SOPs long before anyone has to ask for it.

A short before and afterA six-person machine shop spent the first twenty minutes of most mornings looking for tooling and setting sheets. One 5S pass on the setup bench, a shadow board for the common tools, and a labelled folder for current-revision setting sheets cut that to a few minutes. Nothing was bought in, and the time saved went straight back into running work.
Do I need lean consultants to do any of this?

No. Everything above can be started this week with no external help and no budget. External support can add value later, but it is not a prerequisite to begin.

How do I know if it is working?

Your KPI board is the answer. If the numbers you chose to track are moving in the right direction over a few weeks, it is working.
